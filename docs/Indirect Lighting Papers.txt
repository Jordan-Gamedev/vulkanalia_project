Voxel-based indirect lighting and global illumination (GI) are foundational techniques in real-time computer graphics, primarily used to simulate how light bounces between surfaces without relying on heavy precomputation. [1]  
Here are the key foundational and influential papers in voxel-based indirect lighting: 
1. The Foundation: Voxel Cone Tracing 

• "Interactive Indirect Illumination Using Voxel Cone Tracing" (2011) – Cyril Crassin, Fabrice Neyret, Sylvain Lefebvre, Elmar Eisemann 

	• Why it’s important: This is the landmark paper that popularized voxel-based global illumination. It introduced a method to generate a Sparse Voxel Octree (SVO) dynamically on the GPU. By storing directional radiance and occlusion in the octree, it uses "cone tracing" to approximate diffuse and glossy indirect lighting in real-time. 
	• See also: Crassin's 2011 follow-up insight paper, "Interactive Indirect Illumination Using Voxel-Based Cone Tracing: An Insight". [2, 3, 4, 5, 6]  

2. Industry Adaptation (VXGI) 

• "Dynamic Global Illumination for Games" (2015) – Evan Hart (NVIDIA) 

	• Why it’s important: Presented at GDC, this paper outlines NVIDIA's VXGI (Voxel Cone Tracing) technology. It adapted Crassin's octree approach to use voxel clip-maps, which proved more practical and performant for large, open-world game environments. [5, 6, 7]  

3. Voxel-Based Path Tracing & Multi-Bounce GI 

• "VGI: Voxel-based Global Illumination" (2015) – Tobias Ritschel et al. 

	• Why it’s important: This paper expanded on voxel techniques to create a scalable system that ranges from near-field ambient occlusion to multi-bounce global illumination using instant radiosity and path tracing. [8]  

• "Octree-based Sparse Voxelization for Global Illumination" (2012) – J. Pantaleoni et al. 

	• Why it’s important: Introduced a high-performance, GPU-accelerated sparse voxelization algorithm to calculate single and multi-bounce indirect illumination for complex dynamic scenes. [9]  

4. Memory-Optimized & Hybrid Approaches 

• "Layered Reflective Shadow Maps for Voxel-based Indirect Illumination" (2014) – Y. Dong et al. 

	• Why it’s important: A method that decouples occlusion data (stored in voxels) from lighting/geometric data (stored in layered reflective shadow maps), drastically reducing memory footprint while allowing high-quality dynamic lighting. [10]  

• "Lighting-driven voxels for memory-efficient computation of indirect illumination" (2016) – J. Dong et al. 

	• Why it’s important: Addresses the memory issues of previous voxel algorithms by only generating voxels that significantly contribute to the indirect lighting, resulting in an order of magnitude of memory savings. [11]  

• "Including Reflections in Real-Time Voxel-Based Global Illumination" (2025/2026) 

	• Why it’s important: A recent optimization that combines voxelized scene representations with screen-space ray tracing to achieve high-quality diffuse and specular inter-reflections with better temporal stability and reduced memory overhead. [12]
